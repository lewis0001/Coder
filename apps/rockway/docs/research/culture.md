Research for Rockway. Compiled 2026-05-30. Sources cited inline; uncertainty flagged with *(uncertain)*.

## 1. Language: Llanito (Yanito)

Llanito (also spelled Yanito, pronounced "yah-NEE-toh") is the local vernacular: **Andalusian Spanish heavily mixed with British English, with constant code-switching** mid-sentence. It also carries loanwords from Genoese/Ligurian (500+ words), Maltese, Portuguese, Menorcan Catalan, Haketía (Judaeo-Spanish) and Darija Arabic — a layered history in one tongue. The name's origin is uncertain: possibly "little flatland," possibly a diminutive of the Italian name "Gianni." [Wikipedia: Llanito]

**Demonym:** "Llanitos" is the colloquial demonym for Gibraltarians (the formal term is **Gibraltarian**). Llanito refers to *both* the people and the language. [Wikipedia: Llanito]

> Note: Llanito is described as a **declining mother tongue** — English is now dominant and many younger Gibraltarians speak little of it. Treat it as a cherished heritage register, not the everyday default. [Culture Trip; Wikipedia: Llanito]

### Authentic words & phrases (with meanings)
Anglicisms given Andalusian pronunciation, and calques (literal cross-translations) are the signature of Llanito:

- **¿Qué tal, mate?** — "How's it going, mate?" (classic Spanish greeting + English "mate") [Culture Trip]
- **Te llamo p'atrá / pa'trás** — "I'll call you back" (calque of English "call back" → Spanish *atrás* = "back") [Wikipedia; Culture Trip]
- **No me des la lata** — "Stop annoying me" / "don't give me grief" (lit. "don't give me the can/tin") [Culture Trip]
- **Liqueribá** — liquorice (from "liquorice bar") [Wikipedia]
- **Chinga** — chewing gum [gibraltar-intro / Culture Trip]
- **Beki** — bacon; **keki** — cake; **quecaró** — porridge (from "Quaker Oats") [Wikipedia]
- **Focona** — the Spain–Gibraltar border (from "Four Corners") [Wikipedia]
- **Echegarai** — watchman (from "Check Gate") [Wikipedia]
- **Chakaru** — bouncer / "chucker-out" [gibraltar-intro]

*(Some entries above, e.g. chinga/chakaru, come from blog/Culture-Trip listings rather than academic sources — broadly accepted as authentic but verify before featuring prominently.)*

## 2. Identity & symbols

- **The Rock** — the 426m limestone monolith *is* the identity of Gibraltar; everything orients around it. "The Rock" is used as shorthand for the territory itself. [evendo; common usage]
- **Barbary macaques** ("the apes") — Europe's only wild monkey population, ~300 animals in troops on the Upper Rock. Tailless, so historically (and erroneously) called "apes." **Legend: as long as the macaques remain on the Rock, Gibraltar stays British.** In 1942, with numbers down to seven, Churchill ordered them replenished from Morocco/Algeria. They appear on Gibraltar coins. [Wikipedia: Barbary macaques in Gibraltar; visitgibraltar.gi]
- **The flag** — two horizontal bands, **white over red** (white is double-width, on top), with a **three-towered red castle** in the white band and a **gold key** hanging from the castle gate in the red band. White = peace; red = the people's fortitude/strength. Based on the coat of arms granted by Queen Isabella I of Castile in **1502**. [Wikipedia: Flag of Gibraltar]
- **The key** — "**Key to the Mediterranean**" (and historically "key to Spain"): the gold key symbolises Gibraltar's role as the gateway/lock controlling passage between the Atlantic and Mediterranean. A core, distinctive emblem. [Wikipedia: Flag/Coat of arms of Gibraltar]
- **Gibraltar pound (£/GIP)** — own currency, pegged 1:1 to sterling; reinforces a distinct identity. [Worldatlas]
- **National Day — 10 September** — the city turns **red and white**; flags hang from balconies and windows, people dress in red and white. The colours read instantly as "Gibraltar." [Wikipedia: Flag of Gibraltar]

## 3. Geography & places (for Explore)

- **Upper Rock Nature Reserve** — the protected high ground; home of the macaques and most attractions. [gibraltar.com]
- **St Michael's Cave** — dramatic natural limestone grotto, used as a concert/event venue. [visitgibraltar.gi]
- **Europa Point** — the southern tip, with **lighthouse**; where the Atlantic meets the Mediterranean, **views across to Africa** (Morocco). [evendo]
- **Catalan Bay** — eastern fishing village, locally **"La Caleta"**; small beach away from town. [Wikipedia]
- **Sandy Bay** — restored beach on the east side. [tourism listings]
- **Grand Casemates Square** — main social square at the north end of Main Street; pubs, bars, restaurants; on the site of the oldest fortified part of the city. [Wikipedia: Grand Casemates Square]
- **Main Street** — the pedestrian shopping spine of the old town. [whatsoningibraltar]
- **Ocean Village** — modern marina/leisure quarter near Casemates. [gibraltar.com]
- **Moorish Castle (Tower of Homage)** — early-14th-century (c.1333), the oldest surviving building and a relic of the Islamic period. [TripAdvisor; whatsdownthatstreet]
- **Great Siege Tunnels** — defensive tunnels carved into the Rock during the Great Siege of 1779–83. [evendo]
- **The Skywalk** — glass viewing platform at 340m, higher than The Shard; 360° views over three countries / two continents. [visitgibraltar.gi]
- **Windsor Suspension Bridge** — 71m footbridge over a 50m gorge on the Upper Rock; opened 2016. [gibraltar.com]
- **Dolphin watching** — boat safaris in the **Bay of Gibraltar** (common, bottlenose and striped dolphins). [tourism listings]

### The Levanter (Levante)
The **Levanter** (Sp. *Viento de Levante*) is a humid **easterly wind** through the Strait. When it hits the Rock, moist air rising over the ridge condenses into a distinctive **banner cloud** that streams westward off the top — a visual signature locals know well. Most common **May–October**; brings cloud, humidity and damp, and can blanket the Rock's summit. [Wikipedia: Levant (wind); jollyparrot; meteogib]

## 4. Climate & time

- **Climate:** Mediterranean — **dry, warm summers; mild, damper winters.** Sea-moderated; the Levanter brings humidity and grey "Levanter days." [Met Office; Wikipedia: Levant (wind)]
- **Time zone:** **Central European Time (CET, UTC+1)**, switching to **CEST (UTC+2)** in summer — i.e. **the same as Spain**, one hour ahead of the UK. (Aligned with Spain since the land border reopened in 1982.) [Wikipedia: CET; time.is]

## 5. Tone guidance — authentic vs cringey

**Feels authentic**
- Calling it **"the Rock"**; referring to people as **Gibraltarians** (or "Llanitos" affectionately/knowingly).
- Light, *correct* code-switching as flavour — e.g. a friendly "¿Qué tal, mate?".
- Pride in the **layered, multicultural** identity (British + Spanish + Genoese + Maltese + Jewish + Moroccan).
- **Red & white** and the **key/castle** as quiet identity cues.
- Respect for **National Day (10 Sep)** and the macaque legend as beloved local lore.

**Feels cringey / risky**
- Implying Gibraltar is "part of Spain," or framing it as just a Costa-del-Sol day-trip add-on. Sovereignty is sensitive.
- Calling the macaques "pets," feeding-them jokes, or "monkeys everywhere" clichés (they're wild, protected; feeding is illegal).
- **Mangled or stereotyped Spanglish** played for laughs — Llanito is a real heritage, not a gag. Use sparingly and accurately.
- Generic "British seaside" or generic "Mediterranean resort" tropes — both miss what makes Gibraltar distinct.
- Overusing Llanito as if everyone speaks it daily (it's declining) — keep it as accent/garnish, not the whole voice.

## Design implications for Rockway

**Llanito phrases safe to use (sparingly, as warm garnish — never the core UI text):**
- **"¿Qué tal, mate?"** — a friendly greeting / welcome line.
- **"Te llamo p'atrá"** — playful copy for callbacks/notifications ("we'll call you back").
- **"No me des la lata"** — a light touch for a "snooze/mute notifications" or "stop bugging me" control.
- Demonyms: address users as **Gibraltarians**; **"Llanito"** as an affectionate label, used knowingly.
- Keep all of the above optional and well-spelled; default voice should be clean English with occasional Spanish warmth.

**Identity / visual cues for a distinctive, non-generic UI:**
- **The Rock silhouette** as a hero motif/logo anchor — instantly Gibraltar, ownable.
- **Red-and-white** as a primary palette nod (National Day energy), with the **gold key** as a signature accent/icon — tie into "Key to the Mediterranean" for unlock/access/login flows.
- **Three-towered castle + key** lockup for badges, splash, or an "official" feel.
- A **Barbary macaque** as a friendly mascot/empty-state character (respectful, not slapstick), riffing on the "as long as the apes remain…" legend for loyalty/streak features.
- **Levanter banner-cloud** as a subtle visual for weather/loading/atmosphere — a uniquely Gibraltarian sky.
- Currency in **Gibraltar pound (£/GIP)**; clock/time in **CET (same as Spain, +1 UK)**.

**Place names for Explore:**
Upper Rock Nature Reserve · St Michael's Cave · Europa Point & Lighthouse (view of Africa) · Catalan Bay ("La Caleta") · Sandy Bay · Grand Casemates Square · Main Street · Ocean Village · Moorish Castle (Tower of Homage) · Great Siege Tunnels · The Skywalk · Windsor Suspension Bridge · Bay of Gibraltar (dolphin watching).

**Tone do's & don'ts (quick):**
- DO: pride in layered multicultural identity; "the Rock"; accurate Llanito sprinkles; honour National Day and the ape legend.
- DON'T: imply Gibraltar is "in Spain"; mock or mangle Llanito; treat macaques as pets/feedable; lean on generic British-seaside or Med-resort clichés.
